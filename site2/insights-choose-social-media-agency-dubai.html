How to choose a social media management agency in Dubai

Most brands in Dubai hire an agency for its portfolio and let it go a year later for its results. Here is how to tell the difference before you sign.

By Razal Mohammed · 5 November 2026 · 6 min read

Social media is where people in the UAE decide whether to trust a business. DataReportal's Digital 2026 report puts Instagram's ad reach in the UAE at about 8 million people, roughly 70 percent of the population, with Facebook, TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat reaching millions more. Before someone calls a clinic, books a table or visits a showroom, they usually scroll through its page first.

That is why almost every agency in Dubai offers social media management, and why choosing one is harder than it looks. A good portfolio tells you an agency can make nice posts. It does not tell you whether those posts will bring you enquiries.

What social media management should include

Before comparing agencies, agree on what you are buying. A complete monthly service usually covers:

  • A strategy and a monthly content plan, built around your offers, seasons like Ramadan and the Dubai Shopping Festival, and what your buyers actually ask.
  • Content production: shoot days in Dubai, reels, photos, carousels and stories, not only designs made from stock images.
  • Captions in English and Arabic, written for a UAE audience rather than translated word for word.
  • Posting on a schedule, with your approval before anything goes live.
  • Community management: replies to comments and DMs, with leads passed to your team quickly.
  • Paid social on Meta, TikTok or Snapchat, planned together with the organic content.
  • A monthly report that shows enquiries and sales, not only reach and likes.

Nine checks before you sign

  • Live accounts, not mockups. Ask for two or three accounts the agency runs today and look at the last 30 days of posts, replies and consistency.
  • Who makes the content. Ask whether they shoot in house or rely on stock photos and templates, and ask to see a recent shoot.
  • Arabic done properly. If your buyers speak Arabic, the captions and replies should be written by native speakers.
  • Reply times. Ask who answers comments and DMs, how fast, and what happens at night and on weekends. Slow replies lose paid leads, as we explain in why UAE businesses lose WhatsApp leads.
  • Reporting on enquiries. The report should count clicks to WhatsApp, calls and form sends from each campaign, not only followers.
  • Ads and content under one plan. When one company runs your content and another runs your ads, the message usually drifts.
  • You own everything. Your pages, ad accounts, content files and passwords should stay in your name.
  • Creator compliance. If influencers or UGC creators are involved, check that they hold a valid UAE advertiser permit.
  • One accountable person. You should know exactly who owns your account from the first brief to the monthly report.

Red flags to walk away from

  • Guaranteed follower numbers, or any offer to buy followers or engagement.
  • Your page or ad account set up in the agency's name instead of yours.
  • Feeds that look the same across all their clients.
  • Reports that only show reach, impressions and likes.
  • A price quoted before anyone has asked about your goals, your buyers or your budget.

Questions to ask in the first meeting

  • What would you post in our first month, and why?
  • How will we know in 90 days whether this is working?
  • Who replies to our DMs at 10 pm?
  • Can we see a monthly report you send a current client, with names hidden?

Good agencies answer these specifically and without hesitation. Vague answers now usually mean vague results later.

What the first 90 days should look like

Month one is for the audit, the plan and the first shoot. Month two is about posting consistently and testing the first ads. By month three you should see which formats and offers bring enquiries, and the plan should shift toward them. Social media builds trust over time, so expect steady progress rather than overnight results. If you need leads this week, pair it with search ads.

Questions

It depends on how much content you need, how many shoot days, which languages and whether ads are included. Ask every agency for a written scope with the number of posts, reels and shoot days so you can compare quotes like for like, and keep the ad budget separate, paid to the platform directly.

Most brands in Dubai do well with 12 to 20 posts a month, including reels and stories. Consistency matters more than volume, and three strong reels usually beat ten rushed posts.

A freelancer can work well for posting and design on a small budget. An agency makes more sense when you need shoots, Arabic content, community management and ads handled together by one accountable team.

Expect better engagement within the first month and clear signals on enquiries by the third. Paid social can bring leads sooner, while organic content builds the trust that makes those ads convert.
