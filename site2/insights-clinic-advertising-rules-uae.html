Clinic advertising rules in the UAE: what you can and cannot say

Healthcare is one of the most regulated categories to advertise in the UAE, and the rules tightened again in 2026. Here is a plain guide for clinic owners and marketing teams: who regulates your ads, the claims that get clinics in trouble, and how to plan compliant campaigns that still bring patients in.

By Razal Mohammed · 16 November 2026 · 7 min read

This guide summarises public rules and news reports as of November 2026. It is not legal advice. Rules change, and your licensing authority has the final word, so always check its current standards and circulars before a campaign goes live.

Which authority regulates your clinic's advertising

In the UAE, healthcare advertising follows the authority that licenses your facility. That matters, because each one publishes its own standards and approval process.

  • Dubai: the Dubai Health Authority (DHA) for most private clinics and hospitals in the emirate.
  • Dubai Healthcare City: facilities licensed there follow the Dubai Healthcare City Authority's own advertising policy.
  • Abu Dhabi and Al Ain: the Department of Health Abu Dhabi (DoH), which licenses facilities and publishes the standards they must follow.
  • Other emirates: facilities licensed by the Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP). MOHAP runs licence services for healthcare advertisements, including ads on social media, in directories, at events and through call centres.

The 2026 Dubai rules for social media

In August 2026, DHA sent a circular to private healthcare facilities and professionals on medical advertising on social media, reported widely at the start of September. The core idea is simple: medical advertising must be truthful and evidence based, and any claim about results must be backed up and include the risks.

Absolute or exaggerated claims are not allowed. The words reported as banned include the best, the only, unique, one of a kind, exclusive, safest, incomparable, unprecedented, magic, miraculous, assured success, has no side effects, 100%, absolutely certain, distinguished, famous, pioneer and immediate results.

  • No guaranteed results and no calling a treatment a sure cure.
  • No claiming one service is better than, or safer than, another.
  • No suggesting your doctors are superior to other doctors.
  • No fear based messaging and no unrealistic expectations of a treatment.

Before and after images are only allowed when they show the same patient, are taken with the same lens and have no editing. Retouched results are exactly what the rules are aimed at, and aesthetic clinics have been warned of disciplinary action over edited images.

Any patient photo, video or statement used in advertising needs the patient's written consent, limited to the purpose and time period they agreed to. Live streaming patients under general anaesthesia for promotion is prohibited.

Doctors, staff and influencers who promote a clinic's services need approval from the facility's medical director first. The clinic is also responsible for promotional content filmed on its premises, even when it is posted on a patient's or creator's personal account. Paid creators also need the UAE advertiser permit; see our guide to the UAE advertiser permit.

Outside Dubai

Abu Dhabi and MOHAP licensed clinics follow their own authority's standards, and MOHAP's advertising licences have their own application steps and timelines. The safest working rule across all three is the same: no absolute claims, no unedited proof you cannot back up, consent for every patient appearance, and approval in place before anything is published.

How to plan compliant clinic marketing

  • Lead with education. Doctors explaining a treatment, who it suits and what recovery looks like builds more trust than any claim, and it is far easier to approve.
  • Write claims you can prove. Swap the best clinic in Dubai for specific, checkable facts such as years of experience, qualifications or the treatments you offer.
  • Build approval time into the calendar. Content plans that leave days for medical director sign off and authority approval do not stall when a campaign has to go live.
  • Keep a consent file. Store each patient's signed consent with the content it covers and its expiry date.
  • Brief creators in writing. Give influencers the approved wording, the claims to avoid and the permit check before any visit.
  • Answer enquiries carefully. A WhatsApp AI agent for clinics should only share approved wording about treatments and prices, and hand anything clinical to your team.

Quick checklist before you post

  • Is every claim true, specific and supported?
  • Have you removed words like best, safest, only, guaranteed and 100%?
  • Are risks mentioned where results are discussed?
  • Are before and after photos the same patient, same lens, unedited?
  • Is written patient consent on file for this use and this period?
  • Has the medical director approved it, and does it need authority approval?
  • If a creator is involved, do they hold a valid advertiser permit?
