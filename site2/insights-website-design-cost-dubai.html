Website design cost in Dubai: what actually drives the price

Ask five Dubai agencies to quote for a website and the numbers can differ tenfold. That is rarely about greed. It is about what each one plans to build. Here is what drives the cost, so you can compare quotes like for like.

By Razal Mohammed · 12 November 2026 · 7 min read

Most business owners searching for website design cost in Dubai want a number. The honest answer is that a website is not one product. A five page site for a consultancy, an ecommerce store with two thousand products and a booking platform for a clinic group are different jobs, even if every agency calls them all a website.

We do not publish prices, because every quote we write depends on the points below. What we can do is show you exactly what moves the price up or down, which questions to ask, and the warning signs in a quote that looks too good.

1. What the website has to do

This is the biggest factor by far. Start by writing down the one job the site must do well, then the extras.

  • A brochure site presents the business and sends enquiries to WhatsApp, a form or a call. It is the simplest build.
  • A lead generation site adds landing pages for ads, tracking for every enquiry and fast mobile pages. More planning, more testing.
  • An ecommerce store adds products, payments, delivery rules, stock and order emails. This is where costs climb fastest.
  • A booking or portal site adds accounts, calendars, dashboards or custom logic. This is closer to software than to design.

2. Number of pages and templates

Agencies usually price by unique layouts, not by page count. Ten service pages built on one strong template cost far less than ten pages each designed from scratch. Ask how many templates are included and what happens if you add pages later.

3. Custom design or a theme

  • A premium theme adapted to your brand is faster and cheaper, and fine for many small businesses.
  • A fully custom design takes longer because every layout is designed, reviewed and built for your brand. It pays off when your website needs to look clearly different from competitors.
  • Ask which one you are being quoted for. Two quotes can look similar on paper while one is a theme and the other is a custom build.

4. Platform: WordPress, Shopify or custom code

WordPress suits most service businesses that want to edit pages themselves. Shopify suits most online stores because payments, stock and orders are built in. Custom code makes sense for very fast sites or unusual features. Each platform has its own running costs for themes, plugins, apps or developer time, so ask for those in writing too.

5. Ecommerce details

For an ecommerce website, these points change the quote the most:

  • How many products and variants need uploading, and who writes the descriptions and edits the photos.
  • Which payment gateway you use in the UAE, plus cash on delivery and instalment options.
  • Delivery rules by emirate, courier connections and return handling.
  • Connections to your accounting, inventory or ERP system.

6. Arabic and English

A bilingual site is more than a translation. Arabic pages read right to left, need layouts that mirror properly, fonts that render well, and Arabic copy written for how your customers search. Budget for proper Arabic content, and ask whether the agency builds the Arabic version or just adds a translation plugin.

7. Content, photos and video

Many quotes assume you will provide all text and images. If you do not have them, someone has to write the copy, shoot the products or the team, and edit the footage. Copywriting and a short photo shoot often do more for enquiries than extra design work, so plan for them early rather than filling pages with stock images.

8. Integrations and automation

  • WhatsApp click to chat is simple. A WhatsApp AI agent that answers and books from the website is a separate build.
  • CRM connections, booking calendars, payment links and email marketing tools each add setup and testing time.
  • Analytics and conversion tracking for Google Ads and Meta ads should be included, not an extra. Without it you cannot see which ads bring enquiries.

9. SEO, speed and accessibility

A site that looks good but loads slowly on mobile, or has no proper page titles, headings and structured data, will struggle on Google. Ask whether the quote includes technical SEO setup, page speed work, image compression, a sitemap and Search Console. These are cheaper to do during the build than to fix afterwards.

10. Hosting, domain and care after launch

Hosting, the domain, email, security certificates, backups and updates are yearly costs, separate from the build. Ask who owns the domain and hosting accounts. They should be in your company name. Ask what support is included after launch, and what a small change will cost later.

How to compare website quotes fairly

  • Send every agency the same written brief: goals, pages, features, languages and who supplies content.
  • Ask for the quote split into design, development, content, integrations and yearly running costs.
  • Ask to see live sites they built for businesses of your size, and test them on your phone.
  • Check the timeline, the number of revision rounds and what counts as a change request.
  • Make sure you own the site, the domain, the hosting account and all the files when the project ends.

Warning signs in a cheap quote

  • No mention of mobile speed, SEO setup or tracking.
  • The agency keeps the domain or hosting in its own name.
  • Unlimited pages and revisions for a very low fixed fee, which usually means a template with your logo dropped in.
  • No written scope. If it is not written down, it is not included.
